# Basetherm v2.0.0

Client-side web app that simulates heat transfer from a building basement into the ground and ambient air. It reports heat loss and heat gains per basement floor and dynamic ground temperature fields. It is a ground-up rewrite of the legacy Delphi program.

## Status

**Design approved, scaffolding done, no solver code yet.** Next: install the toolchain, then write the first crates with tests from `validation/bestest-gc/SPEC.md`. Do not change the methods below without discussion. Numbers marked *(est.)* are estimates and must be replaced by benchmark results before they are relied on. Items marked **OPEN** are undecided.

## Scope

In v1:
- 3D conduction in soil on a structured graded grid, with 1D and 2D as the same code path (one or two dimensions collapsed to a single cell).
- Freezing and thawing (enthalpy formulation).
- Surface energy balance driven by hourly climate data.
- Multilayer walls, floors, and insulation meshed explicitly.
- Geometry: **rectilinear (axis-aligned polygon) footprint**, rooms are rectilinear polygons inside it (approved 2026-10-25; the first slice was rectangular only). Basements (any depth) and slab-on-grade (a zero-depth basement) are the same model. Above-grade walls are adiabatic. **Horizontal insulation** at ground level adjacent to the building is supported, with parameters width, thickness, and burial depth (0 = exposed at the surface, with its own absorptivity and emissivity).
- Heat flow **per room** (rooms are axis-aligned rectangles on a level; each room has its own interior temperature as a constant, monthly values, or an hourly series; partitions between rooms are adiabatic) and per level, for floor and walls separately, including the slab on grade. **Sign convention: heat loss (interior to ground) is negative, heat gain is positive.** *(Scope decision 2026-10-25: rooms and per-room results were previously out of scope; the user asked for them.)*
- Periodic steady-state initialisation by a direct solve (mean field plus annual harmonic) and by coarse-in-time warm-up (optional).
- Result range from low, central, and high soil conductivity, derived from soil type and moisture (see Physics).
- Automatic mesh builder and mesh presets.

Out of scope for v1 (snow cover and vegetation are planned for v1.1): moisture transport, groundwater flow, coupling to a room or HVAC model, inter-room heat transfer, server-side computation. A 3D reference solver for corner-correction fitting is not needed, because the main solver is 3D.

Climate files are parsed in the browser and never uploaded.
- **Solver core has no UI or DOM dependency.** It must run in a Web Worker, in Node for tests, and natively for benchmarks.
- **SI units everywhere internally** (m, s, K or °C for temperature, W, J). Convert only at the UI boundary.
- **Never change the physics or numerical method silently.** Discuss first, then record the change here.
- Approximations are documented with their range of validity, and the UI warns when inputs leave it.

## Physics and methods

- **Governing equation:** enthalpy form of heat conduction, `∂H/∂t = ∇·(k∇T)`, with `H(T)` including latent heat of freezing (334 kJ/kg of freezing water; water fraction from soil moisture and density).
- **Soil properties:** separate frozen and thawed conductivity and volumetric heat capacity, a freezing temperature, and a smoothing half-width δ (floor δ ≥ 0.5 K) for the phase-change band. Layered soil is supported in v1.
- **Soil type and moisture:** each soil class (sand, gravel, silt, clay, peat) has `k_dry`, `k_sat`, dry heat capacity, and porosity φ, taken from cited sources. Conductivity follows Johansen–Kersten `k(S)` and heat capacity `Cv = C_dry + S·φ·C_water`, with S the degree of saturation. Latent heat uses the same S. Low, central, and high cases are S ≈ 0.4, 0.7, 1.0 *(to be tuned)*, so conductivity and heat capacity vary together and the spread depends on soil type.
- **Spatial discretisation:** finite volume on a structured, non-uniform (graded) rectilinear grid. Solid materials are assigned per cell, so insulation, slabs, and walls are real cells. Where a layer is thinner than the local cell, it may be represented as a lumped face resistance, and the loss of its thermal mass is documented.
- **Time discretisation:** implicit and unconditionally stable. Baseline is **ADI / Douglas–Gunn splitting** (tridiagonal sweeps), as in the legacy code's progonka method. Time integration is **implicit Euler** throughout. Why: it is the most robust across the freezing front, with no oscillation in the phase-change band and a simple energy-conserving enthalpy update. The cost is first-order accuracy in time, which is acceptable at hourly steps, and the time-step convergence study must show first-order behaviour and a small enough error. Priorities are stability and speed, not a detailed freezing model. Freezing is a non-iterative conservative update with no Picard loop: (1) ADI sweeps solve for temperature with the tangent capacity `C_eff = dH/dT` at the old temperature; (2) the enthalpy that entered each cell is `dH = C_eff (T* - T_old)` (exact for Douglas ADI), so `H_new = H(T_old) + dH` conserves energy exactly; (3) `T = H^-1(H_new)` by a closed-form inverse. `H(T)` is piecewise linear in capacity across the band (quadratic in the band), conductivity blends across the band. **Method change recorded 2026-10-25 (measured, replaces the earlier "lagged secant plus optional repeat"):** a secant predictor from the previous step and a second repeat pass were tried; the predictor changed accuracy by 0.03-0.1 percentage points and caused over-extraction when the step size changed, and once steps are guarded the repeat changed nothing up to 72 h steps (it helped only from 144 h). Instead a step whose result leaves the range that conduction allows (old temperatures and boundary temperatures, beyond the smallest band half-width, 0.5 K) is discarded and redone as two half-steps, recursively (at most 8 halvings), with fluxes averaged so energy stays exact; hourly and daily steps are never split. The lag error is measured in the time-step convergence study. Revisit a second-order scheme only if the convergence study shows the time error is not small compared with the soil-property uncertainty.
- **Linear solver:** for the direct periodic solve. The mean field is real SPD and uses PCG. The annual harmonic `(K + iωC)x = b` is complex-symmetric and uses **COCG** (conjugate gradients without conjugation). Preconditioner: one geometric-multigrid V-cycle with semi-coarsening and a z-line Gauss–Seidel smoother (the vertical cells are finest and most anisotropic). Start with a plain ADI-style preconditioner and add multigrid only if benchmarks show it is needed. Multigrid is also the upgrade path if ADI accuracy is inadequate at high material contrast.
- **Boundary conditions:**
  - Ground surface: `q = h_c(Ta−Ts) + (1−albedo)·GHI + εσ(T_sky⁴−Ts⁴)`. Solar uses **global horizontal** irradiance. Long-wave is exchange between the ground and the sky, with `T_sky = (IR/σ)^¼` from the EPW horizontal infrared column. Evaporation and latent flux are neglected (documented, with validity range). Snow and vegetation are deferred to v1.1; design the surface boundary so a snow layer can be added later without changing the solver core.
  - Far-field sides and bottom: coupled to a 1D undisturbed-ground solution, which has its own spin-up (**optional, see Run options below**: off = adiabatic). Default lateral domain extent is **25 m from the basement**, user-adjustable.
  - Above-grade walls: adiabatic.
  - Interior faces: film coefficient and a per-level interior temperature.
  - Symmetry planes: adiabatic.
- **Symmetry:** used only when the geometry, soil, surface conditions, and per-level interior temperatures are mirror-symmetric (half or quarter domain). It is detected automatically, never assumed silently, and **shown in the results**. If detection fails, the full domain is solved and the user is told why.
- **Warm-up and initialisation** 
  1. Solve the steady mean field and the complex annual-harmonic field directly (linear case, ignoring freezing) to get a periodic initial state. Linearisation: radiation `h_r = 4εσT_m³` with `T_m` the annual-mean surface temperature (iterate 2–3 times); mean-wind `h_c`; harmonic forcing from an FFT of hourly `Ta`, GHI, and `T_sky`; soil frozen or thawed by the local mean temperature (one pass). Wind–temperature covariance is neglected (documented); spin-up corrects the initial state.
  2. Run coarse-in-time warm-up (daily steps, daily-mean forcing) on the same mesh, about 10–20 years, because the thermal bulb under a large building has a time constant of decades (about L²/α).
  3. Run one hourly year for reporting. Report only after the year-to-year change of the annual heat flow (the convergence target, user-set, default 0.5%) is below the threshold.
- **Uncertainty output:** low, central, and high soil moisture and conductivity as parallel cases (see Soil type and moisture). The central case uses the Standard mesh. The low and high cases use a Draft mesh pass.
- **Mesh:** automatic builder with fine vertical cells at the surface (about 5–10 cm), fine cells near walls, floor, and insulation, and strong grading elsewhere. Presets are Draft, Standard, and Fine, defined by grading parameters, not raw cell counts. Mesh error is assessed only in the convergence studies (no runtime gauge).

A numerical change is not done until these pass:
- 1D semi-infinite half-space with sinusoidal surface temperature (analytic).
- Stefan problem for freezing (analytic).
- BESTEST ground-coupling (slab-on-grade) cases, ASHRAE 140 Section 7 (GC10–GC80), reference data from the IEA report (NREL/TP-550-43388, to be verified). These are slab-on-grade only, so basement validation rests on the analytic cases, 1D/2D/3D consistency, and symmetry-reduced versus full-domain runs.
- Case definitions, reference numbers, and the pass rule are in `validation/bestest-gc/SPEC.md` and `reference.csv`. Source files are in `refs/bestest-gc/` (not part of the build).
- **Pass tolerance: 5%.** A BESTEST case passes if the result is within 5% of the reference mean, or inside the reference programs' min–max envelope; either suffices. (ISO 13370 cases were removed from scope.)
- Grid and time-step convergence studies. Target: mesh error under 3% of annual heat loss on the Standard preset for the validation cases, or a documented reason why not.
- Consistency: the direct periodic solve and the time-stepped periodic state must agree for the linear case.

## Architecture

- **Solver core:** N-D structured-grid solver written in **Rust**, compiled to WASM (`wasm-bindgen`). Why: one code base builds to WASM for the browser and natively for benchmarks and tests, with memory safety and no garbage-collector pauses in the hot loop. Threads use `rayon` on WASM (`wasm-bindgen-rayon`), which needs `SharedArrayBuffer` and therefore cross-origin-isolation headers on the host. The worker falls back to a single-thread build on hosts without those headers. **OPEN:** WebGPU as a later option.
- **Modularity:** a Cargo workspace of small crates, with a soft limit of about 300 lines per file, so each piece is easy to fix and cheap to load. Crates: `grid`, `bc` (boundary types, own crate by decision), `conduction` (shared coefficient assembly: `Line`, `Stencil`), `materials`, `freezing`, `adi`, `mg`, `surface`, `farfield`, `periodic`, `climate-epw`, `fluxes`, `wasm-api`, `bench`. The Svelte UI lives in `web/`, one component per file. Each crate gets a short `CLAUDE.md` stub.
- **Budgets (measured, release build, 12-core machine, house basement 12 x 9 m with 25 m margins, `crates/bench`: `basement-timing [draft|standard|fine] [steps] [--freezing] [--sym]`):** f64 throughout. Hard cap 4M cells, warning at 2M. Standard preset: 773k cells; hourly step 144 ms on one thread, **40 ms on 12 threads** (59.7 ms with freezing); a full run (20 daily-step coarse years plus 2 hourly years) about 17 minutes (25 with freezing) instead of 60 (80). With mirror symmetry (quarter domain, 197k cells): 9.6 ms per step, about 4 minutes (6 with freezing). Draft: 186k cells, 9.5 ms. Fine: 3.55M cells, 196 ms, about 81 minutes; its periodic initial state alone takes 82 s (single thread). The periodic initial state at Standard takes 12 s (25 s with freezing). Browsers: current and previous versions of Chrome and Firefox, Safari 16.4+. Threads scale well to 4 (3.1x) and flatten near 4.3x at 12 (memory bandwidth). Not yet threaded: the freezing stepper's per-cell loops, the periodic solve (PCG and multigrid), and `Adi::set_properties` is only partly parallel.
- **Threading (done for the ADI sweeps):** `adi` has a `parallel` feature (rayon, on by default; without it the same code runs on one thread). Each axis pass is split into fixed slabs (x-blocks for the y and z passes, y-slices for the x pass) whose boundaries depend only on the grid; the x pass solves into slab buffers and a second parallel pass copies them in; no `unsafe`. Reductions (group fluxes) are added in slab order. **Results are bitwise identical for any thread count** (test: 1, 2, 3, 5, 12 threads, every temperature and flux compared bit for bit; also identical to the pre-threading checksum). **WASM threads (done):** `wasm-api` feature `parallel` adds `wasm-bindgen-rayon`; `scripts/build-wasm.sh` (default) builds with nightly Rust (`-Z build-std`, atomics, shared imported memory) and the worker calls `initThreadPool(n)` (`?threads=N` on the worker URL overrides `n`; default `hardwareConcurrency`). Checked in headless Firefox, small basement (Draft, 2 coarse + 1 hourly year): hourly output bitwise identical for 1, 2 and 4 threads; wall time 90.8, 63.2, 50.0 s (single-thread wasm is about 2.4x slower than native release on the same run). `scripts/build-wasm.sh` builds both variants (threaded into `web/src/wasm`, stable single-thread into `web/src/wasm-single`); `threads` or `single` builds one. Limits: the threaded module needs `SharedArrayBuffer`, **Fallback (done):** the worker loads the threaded module only if `crossOriginIsolated` and `SharedArrayBuffer` exist, and also falls back if loading or `initThreadPool` throws; otherwise it loads the single-thread build (same results, bitwise). Nightly is needed only for this build; everything else uses stable.
- **Determinism:** results are bitwise identical regardless of thread count (fixed work chunks independent of thread count, reductions in fixed order). No transcendental functions in the hot loop, and never build with `target-cpu=native`. Test tolerances: threaded versus single-thread ≤1e-12 relative; native versus WASM ≤1e-10 relative on fluxes and ≤1e-9 K on temperatures.
- **I/O schema:** versioned JSON project files. 
- **UI:** **TypeScript + Svelte + Vite**. Why: a form-heavy tool with reactive state, small bundle, little boilerplate. Canvas or WebGL for temperature fields. A lightweight charting library for time series, depth profiles, and heat flux per level (library not chosen yet). The solver runs in a Web Worker with progress reporting and cancel, and the UI never touches solver arrays except through a thin typed interface.
- **UI layout (2026-10-25):** a plan canvas (draw the closed outline with straight lines, split rooms with cut lines, select a room, one tab per level) and a side panel with tabs: Rooms (levels, heights, room name and constant or monthly indoor temperature), Case (climate, layered soil, wall, floor and insulation, domain, run, mesh preview), Results (peak loss and gain table per room and total, annual heat-flow graph of the clicked room, monthly temperature sections). The form accepts any simple polygon and polygon rooms; `blockers()` in `web/src/lib/form.ts` lists what the solver cannot run (now only: not a rectilinear polygon).
- **Temperature sections (output only, no physics change):** the final hourly year accumulates monthly mean temperature on the planes y = ly/2 and x = lx/2 (`crates/basement/src/sections.rs`), unfolds mirror symmetry, and masks the building void as NaN; exposed through `RunResult.sectionAxes/longitudinal/perpendicular`.
- **Storage policy:** store hourly heat flux per boundary face group (per level, floor, walls). Store temperature fields only as snapshots (monthly, or on demand).
- **Climate input:** EPW (EnergyPlus weather) files, parsed in the browser, are the primary format (dry-bulb temperature, wind speed, solar radiation columns used for the surface balance). 

- **Rectilinear footprints and partial insulation (approved 2026-10-25):**
  - *Footprint and rooms.* `Building.footprint` is a rectilinear polygon (`None` = the rectangle `[0,lx] x [0,ly]`; `lx`, `ly` are its bounding box, whose corner is the origin). A `Room` is a rectilinear polygon (`Room::rect` for rectangles). Validation (`geom.rs`): even number of corners, alternating horizontal and vertical non-zero edges, no crossing or touching edges, rooms inside the footprint, rooms of one level do not overlap (exact, by coordinate compression).
  - *Cell classification.* Inside the footprint above the depth `d` is void. Wall layers occupy the L-infinity (Chebyshev) distance `dxy` from the footprint, layer boundaries at the cumulative thicknesses, exactly as the rectangle did (square outer corners; at a concave corner the wall is the Minkowski sum with a square). Floor layers cover `dxy <= wall thickness`. Horizontal insulation is the band `wall < dxy <= wall + width`, again by L-infinity distance. For a rectangle every region is identical to before (the existing validation and mesh tests pass unchanged).
  - *Mesh.* Anchor planes at every contour of these distance fields: each footprint edge's extent along the axis moved outward by every wall-layer boundary and the insulation edge (inward and outward for floor-zone widths), plus the room corners and the centre plane. For a rectangle the anchors are the same as before.
  - *Symmetry.* A mirror plane is used only if the footprint itself is symmetric (compared as canonical polygons: collinear corners dropped, counter-clockwise, rounded to 1 um) and every room has a mirror partner of equal shape and schedule.
  - *Partial layers.* `Layer.limit`: wall layers `Depth(d)` (present above depth `d` below grade); floor layers `Perimeter(w)` (present within `w` of the footprint edge, also under the walls) or `Core(w)` (present further than `w` from it). At least one wall layer and one floor layer must be unlimited. Where a layer is absent its cells take: the room (void) if it and every layer inside it is absent (interior insulation that stops, a board on the slab that covers only the core); otherwise the next layer outward; otherwise soil. The room-side face of a stepped wall or floor gets the film coefficient like any other interior face: the underside of a board and a ledge are `ZHi`/`ZLo` faces (the ledge counts in the floor group, the board's underside in the wall group). Void cells outside the footprint in plan (the opened wall strip) belong to the nearest room of the level. Slab on grade never opens a floor layer to the room (the cells take the next layer outward).
  - *Checks.* Face areas of an L-shape, wall and floor limits and the interior-insulation step are asserted exactly in `crates/basement/tests/rectilinear.rs`. Full-year runs (Draft, small domain): an L-shaped basement as one room and as two rooms at the same temperature give -4253.5 and -4253.5 kWh; wall insulation over the top 1 m only gives -5443 kWh of wall loss between none (-8191) and full (-3041).
  - *Regression.* After the change the rectangle cases still pass: the symmetric-rooms case gives -3118.9 kWh per room and -6237.8 kWh whole (recorded earlier: -3118.3 and -6235.3; a 0.02-0.04% difference whose cause was not isolated, well inside the 5% validation margin), quarter versus full domain still agrees, and the sections test passes. The BESTEST validation suite was not rerun (owner asked for a couple of cases only).
  - *Project file* schema version 2: room `poly: [[x,y],...]`, building `footprint`, layer `limit: {kind: depth|perimeter|core, value}`.
- **Soil moisture and class table (approved 2026-10-25):** a soil layer is either explicit properties or `{preset, saturation, freezing}`. `materials::PRESETS` has gravel, sand, silt, clay and peat. Method: Johansen (1975) as presented in Farouki (1981, CRREL Monograph 81-1): grain conductivity `7.7^q 2.0^(1-q)` (quartz fraction `q`), saturated `k_s^(1-n) k_w^n`, dry conductivity of natural soils `(0.135 d + 64.7)/(2700 - 0.947 d)` with `d = 2700 (1-n)`, Kersten number `0.7 log S + 1` (coarse, S > 0.05) or `log S + 1` (fine, S > 0.1), frozen `Ke = S`; grain heat capacity from de Vries (1963). **Correction:** the first slice had the two Kersten coefficients swapped (coarse 1.0, fine 0.7); fixed (verified against Johansen's formulas as quoted in the literature). The quartz fraction and porosity of each class are typical mid-range values chosen here, **not** read from a textbook table: replace them with the owner's cited table when available. With `freezing` the pore water freezes (latent `S n rho L`), otherwise the soil is a constant-property solid.
- **Soil inputs (UI change, 2026-10-26, no solver change):** the soil class and saturation only fill default values; each layer has editable thawed and frozen conductivity, thawed and frozen volumetric heat capacity and latent heat (defaults from `materials`, via the WASM soil API, fetched with `freezing: true`). Changing the class or saturation reloads the defaults and overwrites edits in that layer. One case-wide tickbox, "Simulate the freeze/thaw cycle", replaces the per-layer "Freeze" tickbox: off sends only thawed `k`, `cv` (constant-property soil); on sends frozen values, `latent` and `tFreeze` 0 to the solver's `Material` JSON. Case files from before this change are migrated on load (`simulateFreezing` = any old layer had `freezing`). The WASM must be rebuilt (`npm run wasm`) after any Rust mesher change, or the mesh preview shows the old grid (the far-field edge refinement seen after the removal was a stale build).
- **Far-field edge mesh (checked 2026-10-25):** `Axis::graded` makes the domain end an anchor, so cells shrink back to the finest size at the far-field edges. Test: 4 x 4 m basement, two rooms, Draft, 15 m margin and depth, 9 years, one case. Removing the refined cells within 4 m of each far edge (21141 to 9702 cells, 54% fewer) changes the annual total from -2770.25 to -2769.95 kWh (0.011%), the floor and wall flows by under 0.02% and the peak loss from -407.54 to -407.52 W; within 8 m (7220 cells) the change is 0.4%. Finer cells at the far edges are therefore not needed (they are already the finest size); the lateral edge is coupled to the undisturbed column and carries almost no gradient. **Applied (approved 2026-10-25):** `Axis::graded_ends` takes `fine_ends: [start, end]`; an unflagged end still gets a face but its cells grow to `h_max`. `basement::build_grid` leaves the far lateral ends and the bottom unrefined (the start stays fine at a mirror plane, and at the ground surface); the BESTEST model leaves the far lateral end unrefined and keeps the fixed-temperature bottom fine. Validation after the change: workspace tests, clippy and fmt clean; BESTEST Standard steady and annual cases unchanged (GC10a -0.69% of analytic; steady cases -0.5 to -2.0% and annual cases -0.3 to -2.0% of the reference mean; Standard versus Fine -0.5 to -1.9%, as before). The hourly BESTEST suite and the full-year basement case were not rerun.

- **Run options (requested and recorded 2026-11-03):**
  - *Convergence target* (`options.driftTol`, UI in %, default **0.5%**). **Changed 2026-11-03 (owner request, confirms the earlier "max heat loss" reading):** hourly years are judged on the **peak hourly heat loss** of the whole basement (the most negative hourly sum over all room floor and wall groups): `|P(year n) - P(year n-1)| / |P(year n-1)|`. The run stops when it is below the target. The first hourly year has no hourly predecessor, so at least two hourly years are needed (UI requires "Most hourly years" >= 2; a warm-up year no longer serves as the comparison). The daily warm-up years keep the earlier measure, the change of the annual flow summed over groups relative to the total annual flow, and stop at half of the target. With one hourly year there is no measure (null) and the run is not converged. The reported `drift` and the history record this peak change for hourly years.
  - *Warm-up off* (`options.warmUp = false`): the daily-step years are skipped; hourly years start from the periodic initial state and need more years (raise the hourly-year limit).
  - *Periodic initial state off* (`options.periodicInit = false`, default on; added 2026-11-03): the direct periodic solve is skipped and every column of the 3D domain starts at the undisturbed far-field profile at the end of the far-field year (void cells 0). The warm-up (or more hourly years) then has to build the heat bulb under the building from scratch.
  - *Edge conditioning off* (`options.conditionEdges = false`): the lateral sides and the bottom of the domain are adiabatic in both the periodic initial state and the time stepping (the far-field column is still solved, only for the radiation reference temperature). The domain must then be large enough that the building does not feel the edges, and there is no geothermal heat flux.
  - *History:* every simulated year (warm-up and hourly) records its annual whole-basement flow and change; the results tab plots them against the target.
- **Heat flux sections (output only, 2026-11-03):** the same two centre planes as the temperature sections also accumulate the monthly mean conductive heat flux `q = -k grad T` (W/m2) as a cell-centre vector (along the plane, and down), in `crates/basement/src/sections.rs`. Face flux is the series resistance of the two half cells with `k` at the cell temperature (freezing blend); the cell value is the mean of its faces that have a solid neighbour, so faces to the building void and to the surface are left out (next to them the value is the flux through the opposite face). Mirror unfolding flips the horizontal component. No solver change.
- **Plan drawing (UI, 2026-11-03):** lines are always horizontal or vertical (no orthogonal toggle) and an outline cannot be closed with a diagonal; `blockers()` still catches non-rectilinear outlines from old case files.
